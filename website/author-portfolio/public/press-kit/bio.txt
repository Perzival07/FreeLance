ELARA VOSS  |  AUTHOR BIOGRAPHY
============================================================

SHORT BIO (about 60 words)
------------------------------------------------------------
Elara Voss writes literary fantasy about lost things and the people who carry
lanterns to find them. Her Lanternfall Trilogy, a drowned city, a lamp and the
moths that guard its memories, has sold over four hundred thousand copies and
been translated into twenty-three languages. She lives in Edinburgh with two
cats, three typewriters, and an unreasonable number of candles.

LONG BIO (about 250 words)
------------------------------------------------------------
Elara Voss grew up in a lighthouse keeper’s cottage on the east coast of
Scotland, where the power failed so often that stories were the only reliable
light. Her grandmother told them in the dark, and by the age of nine Elara was
writing them down by torchlight under the blankets.

She studied medieval literature in Edinburgh, then spent a decade working in a
second-hand bookshop on Candlemaker Row, where she read, by her own estimate,
four thousand first chapters and finished about one in ten. That education in
what makes a reader stay shaped everything she wrote afterwards.

Her debut, The Lantern Keeper, was turned down by eleven publishers before it
found a home, and went on to become the first volume of the Lanternfall
Trilogy: a quiet, strange, stubbornly hopeful epic about a drowned city, the
moths that guard its memories, and the woman who tends the last lamp. The
series has sold over four hundred thousand copies and is published in
twenty-three languages.

Between trilogy volumes she wrote Salt & Saint Elsewhere, a standalone about a
fishing village that wakes one morning to find the sea has gone, and her
forthcoming novel, The Cartographer’s Daughter, follows a mapmaker’s child who
inherits a map of places that do not exist yet.

Elara writes only after midnight, always longhand first, always with a candle
on the desk. She is at work on her next book and, she says, is “mostly afraid
of it, which is how I know it’s the right one.”

Based in Edinburgh, Scotland.
