================================================================================
 LUNE & CRUMB — INSPIRATION NOTES FROM 10 REFERENCE RESTAURANT / CAFE SITES
 Compiled: 15 Oct 2026
================================================================================

PURPOSE
-------
Ideas worth borrowing from ten reference sites, filtered so they fit the
existing Lune & Crumb look. Anything that would clash with that look is listed
in section 4 ("Do not adopt").


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. THE AESTHETIC WE ARE PROTECTING (do not break these)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Palette: white / warm cream surfaces (#FFFFFF, #FAF7F1, #FBF9F4), muted gold
  (#D4AF7E, #8C7350), terracotta accent (#B5654A), deep teal WhatsApp
  (#0d655f). Dark mode is supported, so every new block must use the CSS tokens
  in assets/styles.css. No new hard-coded colours.
- Type: Cormorant Garamond (display, italics for emphasis, e.g. "Lune &
  <em>Crumb</em>") + Jost (body, light weights, generous 1.85 line-height).
- Tone: quiet, editorial, artisan, unhurried. Copy is specific and sensory
  ("27-layer cultured butter", "Ovens hot from 06:30"), never shouty.
- Structure: static HTML (index, about, menu, breads, gallery, contact, 404),
  a single WhatsApp conversion path ("Reserve"), no cart or checkout.
- Existing components to reuse before adding new ones: eyebrow + section-title
  + gold-line, highlight-card, review-card track, marquee, live-status-pill,
  rotating-badge, timeline, team-card, stats-grid (count-up), menu-tabs
  (scroll-spy), bread-card + bread-filter, gallery-filter + lightbox, faq-item,
  cta-banner, sticky-bar, scroll-progress, scroll-top-btn, page-hero--photo.

Rule of thumb: borrow the structure, write the copy in the Lune & Crumb voice,
and style it with the existing tokens and components.

1. SITE-BY-SITE NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[1] 6 BALLYGUNGE PLACE: https://www.6ballygungeplace.in/
    Heritage Bengali restaurant group (est. 2003).
    What they do well:
    - Pioneer framing: the story opens with the gap they filled ("the city had
      no standalone Bengali restaurant until 2003"). A strong origin hook.
    - A named food philosophy section ("Rasana Charitamrito") that names two
      signature dishes and links to the menu.
    - Swappable seasonal hero (e.g. Mother's Day thali, "Place Order").
    - Awards wall from 2012 to 2026, used as a trust signal.
    - Catering block with a "Get Quote For Custom Menu" CTA.
    - Blog cards on food history, spices and etiquette.
    - Celebrity testimonials with portraits.
    Take for Lune & Crumb:
    -> Open About with a pioneer or "why we exist" hook, e.g. "Walcot Street
       had no long-fermentation bakery until..." The existing about.html
       timeline already supports this.
    -> Give the baking approach a short name, used as a recurring motif such as
       "The 48-Hour Rule" or "Slow Bread". Use it on index, breads and menu.
    -> Seasonal hero line: keep the video, but swap the live-status-pill text
       and the tagline by season ("Hot cross buns from Good Friday",
       "Stollen now baking"). Change only the copy; the layout stays.
    -> Celebration and wholesale orders: a small "Large orders & celebration
       bakes" block with a WhatsApp CTA carrying a prefilled message (for
       example "I'd like a quote for 40 rolls for an event on __"). This works
       like their catering quote, without needing a form.
    -> Recognition strip: a single, quiet row of press or award mentions in
       Cormorant italics with thin gold dividers. Avoid a dense badge wall.

[2] BOHO THE CAFE: https://bohothecafe.com/
    Instagram-led Kolkata cafe chain, single page.
    What they do well:
    - A three-beat hero line: "Sip, snap, and savour."
    - Alternating image and text blocks ("Awesome vibes" / "Great Food").
    - Location blocks with a wayfinding note ("near the metro station").
    Take for Lune & Crumb:
    -> A three-beat rhythm for a section intro or the marquee:
       "Proof. Bake. Break bread." Set it in Cormorant with italics on one word.
    -> Add a wayfinding line to contact.html, e.g. "Two minutes' walk from
       Bath Spa station, opposite the Saturday artisan market." This is small
       and genuinely useful.
    -> Encourage photos: a gentle "Tag us #LuneAndCrumb" line under the
       gallery. Do not reshape the brand around Instagram.
    Avoid: their vague copy, missing menu, and lack of a clear CTA.

[3] THE MIDTOWN BISTRO: https://themidtownbistro.com/
    Two-location Kolkata bistro.
    What they do well:
    - Stacked, short-line headline with a decorative rule beneath it ("Where
      Every Flavour / Tells A Story"). Our gold-line already does this.
    - A sub-tagline built as two contrasting halves: "Continental Elegance. Pan
      Asian Soul."
    - Three feature pillars directly under the hero (Ambience / Cuisine /
      Coffee).
    - Menu highlights: name, one-line description and price. Five items only,
      then "View Full Menu".
    - Booking runs through WhatsApp with a prefilled message, the same model as
      ours. This confirms our approach works for this kind of business.
    - Closing CTA band with a warm line: "Good food is better when shared with
      good people", plus hours and phone.
    - "Parties & Celebrations" gets its own page.
    Take for Lune & Crumb:
    -> Two-half tagline idea: "French Technique. Somerset Flour." or
       "Slow Dough. Early Mornings."
    -> On index, cap the menu preview at about five signature items, each with
       name, one line and price, then "See the full menu". Check this against
       the existing 4 menu-item rows.
    -> Put hours and phone inside the final cta-banner itself so the reader
       doesn't have to scroll to the footer.
    -> Closing line inspiration: "Bread is better broken together."
    -> Use a distinct prefilled WhatsApp message for each CTA context (reserve
       loaves, celebration cake, wholesale). buildWhatsAppUrl() in
       assets/main.js already centralises this.

[4] I MISS MY CAFE: https://imissmycafe.com/
    Not a restaurant. It is a "virtual cafe" (by Reagan Henke and Evan Cook):
    illustrated cafe scene, ambient-sound sliders (barista chatter, clinking
    cups, rain on windows), an embedded Spotify playlist, dark mode and a
    Pomodoro timer. Body font is "new-spirit" (a warm soft serif).
    (Page is a JS app, so these notes come from its HTML shell and published
    write-ups.)
    Take for Lune & Crumb:
    -> Ambience as content: an optional, muted-by-default "Sounds of the
       bakery" toggle on the gallery video block (oven door, crackling crust,
       espresso grinder). Never autoplay sound.
    -> A curated "What's playing at the counter" Spotify playlist link in the
       footer or the about page "A Morning in..." section. This adds
       personality at no design cost.
    -> Nostalgic, personal copy ("Take a seat and stay awhile") matches our
       unhurried voice. Use it for the 404 page or newsletter blurb.
    Avoid: illustration-heavy UI and Material-style controls. Both clash with
    our photographic, editorial look.

[5] INDIA RESTAURANT: https://indiarestaurant.co.in/
    Four-decade Mughlai restaurant, Kolkata.
    What they do well:
    - Every section header follows one pattern: small kicker ("People Talk",
      "Book Your Table"), then a large heading, then a decorative ornament.
      This is the same system as our eyebrow, section-title and gold-line,
      which confirms our approach.
    - Hero slides each carry one CTA, alternating "View Menu" / "Book A
      Table".
    - "Our Speciality" category cards with photos.
    - Longevity framing ("four decades") and memorable short lines ("We Create
      Delicious Memories", "Eat Good Feel Good").
    - Footer: two addresses with map links, phone per location, weekly hours.
    - Notes that lunch bookings are available.
    Take for Lune & Crumb:
    -> Make eyebrow kickers more conversational and human: "People talk",
       "From the oven", "Come early", "Before it sells out".
    -> Category teaser cards on index (Breads / Pastries / Cakes & Buns /
       Savoury), each linking to its anchor in menu.html#tab. Reuse the
       highlight-card or about-photo-figure style.
    -> Small practical notes near CTAs, e.g. "Reservations held until 11:00"
       or "Sunday stock is limited, so reserve by Saturday".
    -> Add a "Get directions" (Google Maps) link next to the address in the
       footer.

[6] CHOWMAN: https://chowman.net/
    Large Chinese restaurant chain.
    What they do well:
    - Minimal text-led hero with one CTA.
    - Food gallery with each dish photo labelled by name.
    - Stat callouts (4.5/5 rating, 1M+ downloads).
    - "Foodie's Corner / Food Facts" blog cards with tags and dates.
    - Testimonials labelled "What Our Eaters Say", mixing diners and public
      figures.
    - Opening hours in the footer and a back-to-top link.
    Take for Lune & Crumb:
    -> Label every gallery photo with the bake's name (gallery-caption
       already exists, so make sure none are generic).
    -> Use real numbers in the stats-grid: "48h ferment", "27 layers",
       "06:30 first bake", "4.9 Google rating". We already have count-up.
    -> "Baker's Notes": an occasional journal or food-facts strip (why
       sourdough is easier to digest, how to revive a stale loaf, storing
       bread). Good for SEO and fits the editorial tone. Could live as a
       section on breads.html before adding a full blog.
    -> A playful testimonial heading: "Words from Our Regulars" or "Overheard
       at the Counter".
    Avoid: auto-opening promo popups and loud deal banners.

[7] BARBEQUE NATION: https://www.barbequenation.com/
    National buffet chain, conversion-heavy.
    What they do well:
    - "Celebrate an occasion?" with icon tiles (Birthday / Anniversary /
      Family / Office Lunch).
    - Card hierarchy of title, description and short tagline ("Big Feast.
      Small Bill.").
    - Quick-action icon cards (Book / Gift Card / Catering / Takeaway).
    - Takeaway benefits shown as three icons (No Minimum Order, etc.).
    - Nutrition information offered as a download in the footer.
    - Gift or prepaid cards ("Happiness Card").
    Take for Lune & Crumb (refined, not loud):
    -> "Baking for an occasion?" A row of 4 minimal line-icon tiles:
       Birthday cake / Weekend brunch box / Office pastry platter / Gift
       hamper. Each opens WhatsApp with a matching prefilled message. Use the
       same 1.75 stroke icon style as the nav SVGs.
    -> Add a short italic tagline to each bread-card or menu category
       ("Crisp crust. Open crumb.").
    -> Three trust icons under the reserve CTA: "Held for you until 11:00",
       "No deposit", "Collect in 2 minutes".
    -> Allergen and ingredient info: a downloadable or linkable allergen
       sheet in the footer. Useful, and a UK legal expectation (Natasha's
       Law for pre-packed items).
    -> Gift vouchers: a "Bread Club / Gift a Loaf" card bought through
       WhatsApp. This only needs a section, no commerce.
    Avoid: dense promo banners, location selectors, "#1" claims, and
       duplicated CTAs on every card.

[8] PIZZERIA DE CALCUTTA (DotPe storefront):
    https://pizzeriadecalcutta.dotpe.in/store/1/pickup
    A JS-rendered online-ordering storefront, so live content could not be
    captured. Its HTML shell loads Instrument Serif and Playfair together
    with Figtree, a serif-and-sans pairing that mirrors our Cormorant + Jost.
    The general DotPe template uses a pickup/delivery toggle, a sticky category
    bar, item cards (photo, veg marker, price, description, "Add" button), a
    search box and a floating cart summary.
    Take for Lune & Crumb (no cart needed):
    -> A sticky category bar on menu.html: make the menu-tabs stick under the
       navbar while scrolling. Scroll-spy already exists.
    -> Dietary markers: small token-coloured dots or chips (V / VG / GF /
       contains nuts) on menu-item and bread-card. The menu-tag class exists,
       so standardise it.
    -> "Build a reservation": tap items in the menu to add them to a WhatsApp
       message ("2x Country Sourdough, 4x Croissant"), then one button sends
       it. This gives ordering convenience without a cart backend. It would be
       a progressive enhancement in main.js, with localStorage wrapped in
       try/catch.
    -> Mark items as "Sold out today" / "Back tomorrow" (muted style). This
       creates honest scarcity that suits an artisan bakery.

[9] BAYLEAF CAFE: https://bayleafcafe.in/
    The site sits behind a Cloudflare bot challenge ("Just a moment...") and
    returned 403 to automated fetches. No content could be reviewed, and web
    search found no reliable description. Revisit manually in a browser and
    append notes here.
    Lesson anyway: aggressive bot protection can also block Google, social
    link previews and accessibility tools. Keep our static site openly
    crawlable (robots.txt and sitemap.xml are already in place).

[10] OPENHOUSE CAFE: https://openhousecafe.in/
    Kolkata cafe with live events.
    What they do well:
    - Utility bar: click-to-call | centred logo | "Reservation" button.
    - Three info cards right under the hero: Locate Us / Open Hours /
      Reservation. The key facts appear immediately.
    - Story section with a pull quote and a founder signature.
    - Chef profile with portrait and bio.
    - Gallery split into Food / Drinks / Ambience tiles.
    - Events (acoustic nights, etc.) and a blog.
    - Instagram grid (4 square tiles) before the footer.
    - Footer mailing-list signup.
    Things to avoid: blog posts dated 2022 under a 2026 footer (stale), and a
    heading typo. Keep dated content fresh or don't date it.
    Take for Lune & Crumb:
    -> Three slim info cards right below the hero video: "Find us: 14 Walcot
       St" / "Open today: 06:30 to 15:00" (computed by day in JS) / "Reserve:
       WhatsApp". This is the single highest-value addition.
    -> A founder signature: an SVG or script-style signature under the about
       story, plus one pull quote in large Cormorant italics with gold
       quotation marks.
    -> Make the head baker the "chef" figure: a bigger team-card or a short bio
       block ("Our Head Baker").
    -> Community events: "Saturday Sourdough Class", "Starter Swap",
       "Laminating Workshop". This could be a small section on about.html with
       date chips and a WhatsApp "Ask about a place" CTA.
    -> An Instagram strip: 4 to 6 square images from assets/img linking to the
       profile, placed just above the footer. Use the same grid gap as the
       gallery.
    -> The footer-newsletter exists. Keep its copy specific: "Tomorrow's bake
       list, every evening at 6".


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CROSS-SITE PATTERNS (things most of them share)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 a. Kicker, then heading, then ornament for section headers. We already
    have this, so keep it consistent on every page.
 b. Key facts (location, hours, reserve) surface in the first screen or right
    below it.
 c. The menu teaser on the homepage stays short, with a full menu on its own
    page.
 d. Every section ends with one clear next action.
 e. A closing CTA band before the footer, with hours and contact repeated
    inside it.
 f. People behind the food (founder or chef) are shown with names and faces.
 g. Celebrations and large orders get their own path.
 h. Social proof comes in several forms: quotes, numbers, awards, Instagram.
 i. The footer carries full practical info: address with map link, phone,
    hours, socials, legal.

4. DO NOT ADOPT (would break the aesthetic or the UX)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 x Auto-opening promo popups or modals (Chowman).
 x Loud discount banners, "#1" claims, or "Big Feast. Small Bill." value
   messaging (BBQ Nation).
 x Dense multi-row award badge walls with mismatched logo colours (6BP).
   Use one restrained text row instead.
 x Rotating hero carousels/sliders (India Restaurant). Keep the single calm
   hero video.
 x Third-party delivery buttons (Zomato/Swiggy-style). Keep the single
   WhatsApp path.
 x Full cart and checkout UI (DotPe). It is too transactional for this brand.
 x Material-Design controls, illustrated UI, or purple/red UI colours
   (I Miss My Cafe internals).
 x Mega-menus or a long nav with 10+ items (6BP, BBQ Nation). Keep 6 links.
 x Vague mood copy without specifics (Boho). Stay concrete and sensory.
 x Dated blog content that goes stale (Openhouse).
 x Placeholder "#" links for Privacy and Terms (Midtown). Link to real pages
   or omit them.
 x Auto-playing sound of any kind.
 x Any colour or font outside the existing tokens. New sections must work
   in both light and dark mode.
